## 2. Data Retention

### Student personal data in Supabase (`exam_attempts`)

- **How long kept:** Indefinitely. There is no automated deletion, no expiry column, no scheduled job, and no retention policy documented anywhere in the codebase.
- **Auto-deletion:** Absent. No database-level TTL, no `pg_cron` job, no application-level scheduled cleanup.
- **After exam completion:** Data remains in `exam_attempts` with `status = 'submitted'`. Teachers can manually delete individual submissions (`submissions.html` line 1689: `sb.from('exam_attempts').delete().eq('id', _deleteAttemptId)`) or delete the entire exam and all its attempts (`submissions.html` line 1726: `sb.from('exam_attempts').delete().eq('exam_id', id)`). No documented retention period exists and no automated process enforces deletion.
- **Session log:** Stored indefinitely in `exam_attempts.session_log` alongside answers and scores. Session log events include timestamps, behavioral labels (e.g., "Copy attempt", "Paste attempt blocked", "Screenshot key pressed") and detail strings. No separate retention period applies.

### Teacher data in Supabase (`teachers`, `auth.users`)

- **How long kept:** Indefinitely. No account deletion flow is present in any page reviewed.
- **Auto-deletion:** Absent.

### Student browser localStorage

- **Flags retained after exam:** `terminated_exam_{shareId}` (value `'1'`) and `submitted_{shareId}` (value `'1'`) are set on the student's device and never cleared by the application. They persist until the student manually clears browser storage.
- **No mechanism** exists for the student to trigger clearing of these localStorage entries, nor are students informed that their device retains these flags.

### Emails sent via Resend

- Resend retains email logs and delivery metadata per its own privacy policy and data retention settings. The testflo platform has no control over or visibility into Resend's retention period beyond what Resend's DPA (if signed) would specify.

## 4. Third Party Data Flows

### 4.1 Resend (transactional email)

**What data is sent:**
- Student name (`to_name`, `student_name`) — in all three email types (results, retake, invite)
- Student email address (`to_email`) — used as the email recipient
- Student exam answers (MCQ and open text) — in results emails when teacher selects "Full Report" mode (`send-email/index.ts` lines 167–247)
- Student score and submission date — in results emails
- Teacher name and email — in all email types as contact information
- Exam title, access code (in retake and invite emails)

**Under what conditions:** Triggered manually by the teacher from `submissions.html` when they click "Send results", "Send retake link", or "Send invitations". The student receives no advance notice that their results will be sent via a third-party email service.

**DPA status:** No evidence of a signed Data Processing Agreement with Resend is referenced anywhere in the codebase, documentation, or audit files. Resend offers a DPA but there is no indication one has been signed. Under GDPR Article 28, a DPA is legally mandatory before a sub-processor processes personal data on behalf of a controller.

**Evidence:** `supabase/functions/send-email/index.ts` lines 41–51 show the direct call to `https://api.resend.com/emails`. `submissions.html` lines 1499–1527 and 2047–2057 show what data is included.

### 4.2 Supabase (database, authentication, edge functions)

**What data is processed:** All student personal data described in Section 1 is stored in Supabase PostgreSQL. Supabase also handles teacher authentication via Supabase Auth (`auth.users`). Edge functions run on Supabase infrastructure and process student name, email, answers, session log, and session tokens.

**Who has access:** The testflo account owner (dwayne.swanson@gmail.com per MEMORY.md). Supabase staff have access to hosted infrastructure per their terms of service.

**Infrastructure location:** Supabase is a US company (Supabase Inc.); project `toxgihdyfzdymgidgvaq` is hosted on a region not identified in the codebase. If hosted outside the EU/EEA (likely AWS us-east-1 by default), this constitutes an international data transfer requiring appropriate safeguards (SCCs or adequacy decision) under GDPR Chapter V. No such transfer mechanism is documented.

**DPA status:** Supabase offers a DPA via their website. No evidence in the codebase that one has been signed or is in place.

**Evidence:** `SUPABASE_URL` = `https://toxgihdyfzdymgidgvaq.supabase.co` appears in multiple files (`exam-onboarding.html` line 461, `login.html` line 270, `submissions.html` line 959).

### 4.3 GitHub Pages

**What data is logged:** Every HTTP request from a student's browser to `https://exam.test-flo.com` is served by GitHub Pages (via Fastly CDN). GitHub Pages access logs include student IP addresses, user-agent strings, request timestamps, and page paths. This is standard CDN behaviour.

**Control:** GitHub's privacy policy and data retention rules apply. The testflo operator has no control over these logs. GitHub may use aggregated telemetry data per their terms.

**DPA status:** No evidence of a GitHub DPA in place. GitHub offers a DPA for enterprise plans; it is unclear whether the current plan includes one.

**Evidence:** `SESSION_AUDIT.md` line 4 confirms the site is hosted at `dwayneswanson-ecdp/exam-builder-admin` on GitHub Pages.

### 4.4 Google Fonts

**What data is logged:** `exam-engine.html` lines 11–12 load the Inter font from `fonts.googleapis.com` with a `<link rel="preconnect">`. Every student who loads the exam page sends their IP address and user-agent to Google servers as part of this font request. Google may log this data per their privacy policy.

**Student context:** The student has not consented to their data being sent to Google before they even interact with the exam login form. Under French CNIL guidance, loading resources from US servers (Google) without consent or a valid transfer mechanism is problematic, particularly when the data subject is a student who may be a minor.

**Evidence:** `exam-engine.html` lines 11–12.

### 4.5 EmailJS (vestigial, partially active)

**Risk:** Even without an active send call, the SDK load and init constitute a data transfer to a US third party (EmailJS / HubSpot) without disclosure or consent. This is a zero-value data transfer — it provides no benefit to the platform but creates a compliance liability.

**Evidence:** `exam-engine.html` lines 369, 1415–1417.

## 5. Missing GDPR/CNIL Requirements

### 5.1 Privacy Notice / Privacy Policy
- **What is missing:** No privacy policy exists anywhere in the application. Not on the student exam page, not on the teacher login page, not linked from any page.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 13 requires that data subjects be provided with information about the controller's identity, purposes and legal basis for processing, categories of data, recipients, retention periods, and their rights — at the time data is collected.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.2 Cookie / Local Storage Disclosure
- **What is missing:** Students are not informed that the platform stores persistent data in their browser's localStorage (flags `submitted_{shareId}`, `terminated_exam_{shareId}`, session token, shuffle map). No cookie banner exists.
- **What is required:** The French ePrivacy Law (Article 82 of the Data Protection Act implementing EU Directive 2002/58/EC) and CNIL guidance require that users be informed of and consent to the storage of non-strictly-necessary data on their device. Functional state flags strictly necessary for the exam session may qualify as strictly necessary, but `submitted_` and `terminated_` flags that persist after the session ends are not session-functional.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory under French law for non-strictly-necessary storage.

### 5.3 Lawful Basis Documentation
- **What is missing:** No legal basis is documented or disclosed for any processing activity. The platform relies on implied legitimate interest or contractual necessity but neither is stated.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 6 requires a documented lawful basis. GDPR Article 13 requires this basis be disclosed to data subjects.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.4 Data Processing Agreements with Sub-processors
- **What is missing:** No DPAs are evidenced with Resend, Supabase, GitHub, Google, or jsDelivr/Cloudflare.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 28 requires a written DPA with every sub-processor (data processor acting on behalf of the controller).
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.5 International Data Transfer Mechanism
- **What is missing:** Student personal data is transferred to Supabase (US company), Resend (US company), GitHub (US company), Google (US company), and EmailJS (US company) without any documented transfer mechanism (Standard Contractual Clauses, adequacy decision, etc.).
- **What is required:** GDPR Chapter V requires a valid transfer mechanism for data leaving the EEA. France/EU does not yet have a US adequacy decision in force for commercial SaaS (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework exists but must be explicitly relied upon by each processor).
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.6 Right to Erasure (Right to be Forgotten) — Student
- **What is missing:** There is no mechanism for a student to request deletion of their personal data. No contact point, no form, no documented process.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 17 grants data subjects the right to erasure. Controllers must have a process to honor such requests within one month. Technically, teacher-initiated deletion exists in `submissions.html` (lines 1689, 1726) but this is a teacher-only tool — the student has no way to exercise this right themselves.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.7 Right to Access — Student
- **What is missing:** No mechanism exists for a student to request access to the data held about them.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 15 grants data subjects the right to obtain a copy of their personal data. No contact point or process is documented.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.8 Right to Rectification — Student
- **What is missing:** Students can only correct their name and email before the exam starts (by clicking "Edit" on the confirmation screen). After submission, no mechanism exists to correct incorrect data (e.g., a student who mistyped their email cannot fix it post-submission).
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 16.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory (the right exists; a process to honor it is missing).

### 5.9 Data Retention Policy
- **What is missing:** No retention period is defined or enforced for any data. Student submissions remain indefinitely until a teacher manually deletes them.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 5(1)(e) (storage limitation) requires that personal data be kept "no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed." CNIL recommends defining and documenting specific retention periods.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory (principle); specific periods are determined by the controller's documented policy.

### 5.10 Session Log Disclosure Specificity
- **What is missing:** The acknowledgement checkbox on the exam confirmation screen (`exam-engine.html` line 298) says "I understand that my activity generates a session journal transmitted to my instructor" — but does not specify what events are logged. The monitoring disclosure box lists "window exits, tab changes, fullscreen exits, screenshot attempts, copy-paste attempts" (`exam-engine.html` lines 613–623). However, the actual `logEvent()` calls log additional events not listed, including translation detection (`exam-engine.html` line 1402), student return after warning (line 1094), and the exact content of text copied from answer boxes (line 901: "Answer copied from text box" with character count). Logging the content of copied text goes beyond what is disclosed.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 5(1)(a) (transparency) and Article 13 require that the data subjects be informed of the actual scope of data collected.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.11 Informing Students About Third-Party Email Delivery
- **What is missing:** Students are not informed that their name, email address, exam score, and answers will be transmitted to Resend (a US third-party) when the teacher sends results.
- **What is required:** GDPR Article 13(1)(e) requires disclosure of recipients or categories of recipients of personal data.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory.

### 5.12 Vestigial EmailJS SDK on Student-Facing Page
- **What is missing:** EmailJS SDK is loaded and initialised on `exam-engine.html` despite being unused. This constitutes a data transfer (IP, user-agent) to a US third party without purpose or disclosure.
- **What is required:** The SDK should be removed entirely. Any third-party SDK loaded on a page that processes personal data requires a legal basis.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory (remove unnecessary third-party data transfers).

### 5.13 Google Fonts Loaded Without Consent on Student-Facing Page
- **What is missing:** `exam-engine.html` loads Inter from Google Fonts via CDN on every page load, transmitting the student's IP to Google before any interaction.
- **What is required (CNIL position):** The CNIL and the German DPAs have both issued decisions requiring consent before loading Google Fonts from Google CDN servers. The CNIL's January 2022 formal notice and subsequent fine against a French website for Google Fonts loading without consent establishes clear precedent. Self-hosting fonts eliminates this issue entirely.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory under CNIL/French enforcement posture.

### 5.14 No Data Protection Officer (DPO) Designation or Contact
- **What is missing:** No DPO contact is provided anywhere. No contact address for data protection queries is disclosed.
- **What is required:** If the platform processes personal data at scale or processes sensitive categories, a DPO may be required under GDPR Article 37. Even if not mandatory, a data protection contact point is required by Article 13(1)(a). CNIL requires French controllers to be identifiable.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Contact point: legally mandatory. Formal DPO designation: depends on processing volume.

### 5.15 No Record of Processing Activities (ROPA)
- **What is missing:** No Records of Processing Activities document exists (required by GDPR Article 30 for organisations with more than 250 employees, or for any organisation processing personal data "regularly" or involving special categories or criminal data).
- **What is required:** If the platform scales to regular processing, a ROPA must be maintained.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Mandatory if processing is regular; best practice at any scale.

### 5.16 No Consent Mechanism for Minors
- **What is missing:** The platform explicitly targets students in educational institutions. French law and CNIL guidelines require parental consent for minors under 15 for online services. No age verification, no parental consent mechanism, and no check that the teacher's institution has obtained appropriate authorisations exists.
- **What is required:** French Data Protection Act Article 45 (implementing GDPR Article 8) sets the age of consent at 15 in France. Below this age, parental consent is required.
- **Mandatory or best practice:** Legally mandatory if any students under 15 use the platform.

## 7. CNIL-Specific Notes

### 7.1 Google Fonts — CNIL Enforcement Action (January 2022)
The CNIL issued a formal notice in January 2022 and subsequently fined a French website for loading Google Fonts directly from Google CDN servers without user consent. The CNIL's position is that transmitting a visitor's IP address to Google in this way is a transfer to a third country without an adequate level of protection, requiring either prior consent or a strictly necessary exemption (which does not apply to decorative font loading). `exam-engine.html` loads Google Fonts on every student page load. This is one of the highest-certainty CNIL compliance violations in the codebase and the remediation (self-hosting) is straightforward.

### 7.2 Consent Age for Minors (Article 45, French Data Protection Act)
France sets the age of digital consent at 15, lower than the GDPR Article 8 default of 16. If any students under 15 use the platform — which is plausible given the educational context (collège, lycée) — parental consent is legally required. The platform collects student names and emails, which are personal data, with no age check. The CNIL has published specific guidance on consent for minors in digital services ("Mineurs en ligne"). Institutions (écoles, collèges, lycées) using the platform should be required to attest that they have obtained or do not require parental consent for their student cohort.

### 7.3 CNIL Cookie Guidelines (2020 Updated Guidelines)
Under CNIL's 2020 guidelines (aligned with the ePrivacy Directive), any non-strictly-necessary storage on a user's device requires prior consent. The `submitted_{shareId}` and `terminated_exam_{shareId}` localStorage flags stored on the student's device survive the exam session and are used to block future access — they are not purely session-functional. The CNIL may consider these as non-strictly-necessary storage requiring consent or at minimum a clear disclosure. Strictly session-functional cookies (e.g., `examAttemptId_`, `examSessionToken_`, `examDeadline_`) that are cleared on submission are more defensible as strictly necessary.

### 7.4 CNIL Notification Thresholds
The CNIL does not require advance notification for most processing activities (this was abolished by GDPR). However, if the platform involves automated decision-making with significant effects on data subjects (e.g., automatic exam submission and permanent session lockout based on behavioral monitoring), GDPR Article 22 and CNIL guidance may require disclosure of automated decision-making and the right to human review. The anti-cheat system that auto-submits and permanently locks a student's exam session after 3 detected exits constitutes automated processing with a significant effect on the student's assessment outcome. A data subject rights mechanism (right to contest automated decisions) must be made available.

### 7.5 Security Breach Notification (CNIL Article 33/34)
The platform processes personal data of students who are likely French residents. In the event of a data breach, the CNIL must be notified within 72 hours under GDPR Article 33. There is no documented incident response plan, breach notification procedure, or designated contact for breach reporting in the codebase or documentation.

### 7.6 CNIL Registration / Délégué à la Protection des Données
Under GDPR (which replaced the pre-2018 CNIL registration regime), French organisations no longer register their processing with the CNIL. However, if the platform operates for a French institution or processes data of French residents in the context of a French establishment, the platform operator is subject to the CNIL's jurisdiction. The operator should assess whether they qualify as an independent controller or a data processor acting on behalf of institutions. If the latter, each institution is the controller and must have its own ROPA and legal basis documentation. This controller/processor boundary is not defined anywhere in the platform.
